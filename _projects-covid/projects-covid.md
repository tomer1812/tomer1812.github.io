**During the COVID-19 pandemic, public-health decisions had to be made before all the evidence was available.** At the **Segal Lab, Weizmann Institute of Science (2020–2021)**, we devised national symptom surveys to help identify outbreak regions and developed models to prioritize testing when capacity was limited. We also developed models and analyses to help understand hospital demand, patient outcomes, and the population-level response, and studied changes in pandemic dynamics following vaccine rollout.

## When hospital pressure changes patient outcomes

A central question was whether rising hospital load was associated with worse outcomes, beyond differences in who was being admitted. We combined multistate survival modeling with nationwide records of **22,636 hospitalized patients**. The models represented patients' movement between clinical states and compared observed mortality with predictions accounting for patient characteristics.

Periods with more than 500 severe or critical patients hospitalized nationally were associated with increased mortality compared with lower-load periods. This provided evidence that the consequences of hospital pressure could emerge below the official capacity threshold of 800. The finding describes an association from observational data.
